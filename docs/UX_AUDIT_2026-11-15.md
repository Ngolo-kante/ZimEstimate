# ZimEstimate UX audit, 15 November 2026

**Overall: critical issues.** The product is clear about what it is for, and the home page says it well. But it switches off zoom for everyone, a lot of its text is too faint or too small, and the working screens are built for a desktop: on a phone a 15-line project needs up to 6,900px of scrolling per view. Nothing here needs a redesign. Most of it is fixable in the shared pieces (buttons, inputs, tables, the phone baseline).

Each finding has an ID so it can become a ticket.

## How this was checked

- **Screens:** 37 screens at 390×844 (phone) and 1440×900 (desktop), on the current `unified` code, signed in against the local stand-in database with made-up data. No live data was touched.
- **Projects used:** an empty draft, a normal 15-line build, and a worst-case build with a long name, a long address, 1,250,000 bricks, a $385,000 labour line, an unpriced item and a long file name.
- **Measured automatically on every screen:** text size, contrast (computed from real colours), tap-target size, input size, unnamed controls, sideways overflow, console errors.
- **Read by eye:** 22 screens as full-page screenshots (17 phone, 2 desktop, 3 worst-case).
- **Read in code:** the phone baseline (viewport, hover, press states, safe areas) and how the app animates.
- **Emil Kowalski's skills:** `mobile-native` for the phone baseline, `emil-design-eng` for press, hover and transition details, `break-ui` for the worst-case data, and `find-animation-opportunities` for the motion section. Findings that come from one of them say so in the skill table at the end.
- **Guidelines:** Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, cited as `page › heading`. ZimEstimate is a web app, so only the foundations apply (accessibility, colour, type, layout, writing, feedback), not Apple's navigation conventions.

**Limits.** A phone-sized browser is not a phone: tap delay, the address bar and the keyboard need a real device. Wizards were checked on their first step only. Hover and focus states were not captured. Marketplace, admin, supplier and contractor screens were measured but not read.

## Craft notes

- **Point of view.** The home page has one: "Know the cost before you lay a brick", with a budget box you can type into straight away. That is the thing the product would be remembered by. The workspace has none. It is white cards, blue buttons and grey labels, the same as any dashboard. That is acceptable for a tool people work in, but it should then be quieter and denser than it is.
- **Boldness is spread thin.** Blue is the primary button, every link, the bought quantities, the progress bars and the active tab. Save the filled blue for one action per screen.
- **Remove one accessory.** Overview: the "Pro Tip" card. BOQ: the cost-split donut above the table. Usage: the 12 materials with nothing used yet, shown in full.

## What works

- The home page, including the budget check and the "start from what you have" choices.
- Compliance: a clear summary, sensible grouping, and it fits a phone.
- Buying on desktop: one compact row per line with the numbers in columns. The phone view should follow this pattern.
- Empty states say what to do next ("No labour items yet · Add labour or service items").
- The tablet sidebar now collapses to 84px, leaving 670px for content.
- Views and stages are in the URL, so refresh and Back keep your place.
- No sideways scrolling on any of 74 captures except C3, and no script errors on any screen.

## Where motion would help

Found with Emil Kowalski's `find-animation-opportunities`, which filters every candidate by how often it is seen, why it moves, how fast, and whether it gets in the way. The curves below are his tokens. The app needs less motion, not more. Five places would gain from it.

| # | Location | Today | Purpose | Frequency | Suggested motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `src/components/ui/Button.tsx:50` | No press feedback; `transition: all 0.2s ease` | Feedback | Tens a day | `:active { transform: scale(0.97) }`, `transition: transform 160ms cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`. Hover inside `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`. |
| 2 | `src/styles/projects-list.css:885` (phone card menu sheet) | Slides up over 300ms with a generic curve; disappears instantly | Spatial consistency | Occasional | Enter `translateY(100%)` → `0`, 300ms `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`. Exit the same way in 200ms. Use a transition, not keyframes, so it can be interrupted. Fade only under reduced motion. |
| 3 | `src/styles/projects-list.css:604` (card dropdown) | Fades in from nowhere | Spatial consistency | Occasional | `transform-origin: top right`, from `scale(0.97)` and `opacity: 0`, 150ms `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`. |
| 4 | `src/components/projects/StageBOQSection.tsx:326` and `procurement/ProcurementItemsTab.tsx:57` | Sections snap open and shut | Preventing a jarring change | Occasional | Height and opacity, 200ms `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`; opacity only under reduced motion. |
| 5 | `src/components/projects/list/SavedNotice.tsx` | The "saved" notice appears instantly | Delight (rare) | Rare | `@starting-style { opacity: 0; transform: translateY(8px) }`, 300ms `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`. |

**Considered and rejected**

- Scroll-reveal on the workspace, Buying and the list (`design-tokens.css:243`). **Rejected: seen on every visit.** Remove it there (H11).
- Switching workspace views from the sidebar or dock. **Rejected: core navigation, many times a day.**
- Drawing the cost-split donut or progress bars. **Rejected: data people are reading.**
- Cards lifting on hover (`project-workspace.css:448`, `projects-list.css:537`). **Rejected: decoration on working cards, and it sticks on touch.**
- The stage strip's smooth scroll. **Rejected: it causes M6.**

## Where shadcn fits

The official shadcn skill is installed. ZimEstimate has no `components.json` yet. It already has Tailwind 4, `clsx` and `tailwind-merge`, but most screens are styled with styled-jsx and the app's own `src/components/ui`.

- **Good fit:** Table for the BOQ, Buying, purchases and usage lists (H5, H8); Select, Input and Field for M3 and H2; Dialog and Sheet for the purchase form and phone menus; DropdownMenu for card menus; Tabs and Badge for M4; a toast with Undo for H7.
- **Cost:** it adds npm packages (Radix or Base UI, `class-variance-authority`) and a second styling approach alongside styled-jsx until screens are moved over.
- **Suggestion:** adopt it for tables and form controls first, one view at a time, starting with Buying on a phone. Keep the app's own colours and type by mapping the existing tokens. Decide this in planning, since it changes how every later ticket is built.

## Which skill does each fix

Emil Kowalski's skills carry the rule behind most of the feel and phone findings, and they are the ones to load when building each fix.

| Finding | Skill | The rule it applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 zoom off, H2 input zoom | `mobile-native` | "Never disable zoom." Fix the cause instead: inputs at 16px minimum. |
| H1 tap targets | `mobile-native`, `apple-hig-review` | Touch comfort; press feedback and `touch-action: manipulation` on every tappable element. |
| H3 press and hover | `emil-design-eng`, `mobile-native` | "Buttons must feel responsive": `scale(0.97)` on `:active`, 100–160ms. Hover only inside `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`. Never `transition: all`. |
| H11 slow fade-in on work screens | `remove-animations` | Motion on high-frequency surfaces is removed, not retuned. |
| M9 phone basics | `mobile-native` | Its baseline: `viewport-fit=cover` with safe-area padding, `100dvh` for the app shell, `overscroll-behavior`, tap highlight off, a browser-bar colour per colour scheme. |
| M6 BOQ jump, stage strip | `gestures`, `mobile-native` | A horizontal strip owns one axis only (`touch-action: pan-x`, scroll snap); it must not move the page. |
| C3, H8, H9, M7 worst-case breaks | `break-ui` | Its failure signatures: `min-width: 0` on text columns, `overflow-wrap: anywhere`, `flex-shrink: 0` on icons, `Intl.NumberFormat`, never truncate amounts, middle-truncate file names. Keep the worst-case project as the regression check. |
| Motion items 1–5 | `animate`, then `/review-animations` | Decide in order: should it move, why, which properties, which curve and duration, how it exits. Only `transform` and `opacity`; exits faster than entrances; reduced motion keeps the fade and drops the movement. |
| Motion item 2 (phone sheet) | `gestures` | Sheets follow the finger, dismiss on a flick, and can be caught mid-motion. Consider Vaul, his drawer library, through `/pick-ui-library`. |
| Motion item 4 (sections opening) | `choreograph` | One entrance per container; short, uneven stagger if several things move. |
| H7 undo toast | `ask-sonner` | Sonner, his toast library, has an action button for Undo. shadcn's toast is Sonner. |
| H5, M3 tables and form controls | `shadcn`, `emil-design-eng` | shadcn supplies the components; Emil's rules set how they press, open and close. |
| H4, H6, H10 layout order | `apple-hig-review`, `/prototype` | Try two or three arrangements side by side before choosing one. |
| Whole-app motion plan | `improve-animations` | Audits all 83 keyframes and 130 `transition: all` uses and writes one plan per fix. |

After the first fixes land, `/learn-taste` can turn the decisions made here (the 44px floor, one money format, "Labour", four status colours) into written rules or lint checks so they hold.
